Wedding planning checklist printable — twelve months, twelve short lists

Last updated: November 12, 2026

Nine to twelve months out, the advice online adds up to one 300-item monster list — which is exactly the kind of list nobody works through. This version is scoped differently: each month owns a handful of decisions, and the checklist tells you what to stop thinking about until its month arrives. Start at your month, ignore everything before it.

How to use this checklist

Find your months-out number, read only that block, do the handful of things in it, and close the tab. The whole trick to non-overwhelming wedding planning is refusing to think about months that aren't yours yet. Everything below is a convention, not a rule — your venue's and vendors' timelines override this.

12 months out → 6 months out

  • Month 12–10: venue and date locked (they gate everything), rough budget with a running total, guest count draft, engagement photos if you want them.
  • Month 9–8: photographer, caterer, and officiant booked; save-the-dates addressed. Book vendors with question scripts, not vibes — see the Vendor Question Scripts.
  • Month 7–6: dress bought (alterations eat months), registry started, hotel blocks reserved, wedding party formally asked.

6 months out → 3 months out

  • Month 5: invitations ordered, flowers and cake booked, menu tasting.
  • Month 4: dress fitting one, suit sorted, ceremony readings chosen, day-of transportation confirmed.
  • Month 3: invitations mailed, final dress fitting scheduled, marriage license paperwork looked up (some counties have waiting periods).

Final 3 months

  • Month 2: RSVPs chased (yes, the silent 20%), seating chart drafted, vendor payments scheduled against payday, song list with the DJ/band.
  • Month 1: final fittings, vendor confirmations in writing, and — the month's real job — the day-of timeline built and sent to every vendor and the wedding party.

The last 2 weeks

Confirmations only: reconfirm arrival times with every vendor, pack the emergency bag (sewing kit, snacks, flats, chargers, cash for tips), hand a trusted person the vendor day-of contact list, and stop adding tasks. The plan is done; the job now is showing up.

FAQ

When should you start planning a wedding?

Twelve months out is comfortable; nine is workable if you move fast on the venue. Book the venue and date first because they decide the guest count, the budget shape and every vendor's availability — everything else chains off that decision.

What are the most important things to book first for a wedding?

Venue and date, then photographer, then caterer. The venue gates everything — capacity, date, budget. Photographers and good caterers book single dates and disappear a year out. Everything else has more supply and can be chosen later.

How far in advance should you send wedding invitations?

By convention: save-the-dates around six months out, formal invitations about eight weeks before, with RSVPs due a month out so the final count reaches caterers in time. Destination weddings run earlier. These are workable defaults, not rules.

What should a wedding budget include that people forget?

Vendor tips, dress alterations, invitation postage, day-of snacks for the wedding party, the marriage license fee, overtime rates, cake-cutting and corkage fees, and buffers for weather plans. The forgotten lines are rarely big — they're just never on the first list.
