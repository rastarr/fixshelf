Avalanche or snowball?

On pure maths, avalanche wins — kill the highest interest rate and every dollar works harder. On human maths, snowball wins more often — the early killed balances are the motivation that keeps the plan alive at month four. The tracker lays both out with your real numbers so the choice is yours and visible. Printable PDF, instant download.

What is the debt snowball method?

Listing every debt by balance, paying minimums on all of them, and throwing every spare dollar at the smallest balance until it's gone — then rolling that payment into the next smallest. The wins come fast and early, which is why it works for motivation even though the avalanche method is cheaper on interest.

How do you track debt payoff progress?

One row per debt per month: balance, payment, remaining. The point is visibility — a filled-in log shows the trend the bank app hides, and the debt-free date printed at the top of the page moves closer every time a payment lands. Progress you can see is progress you keep making.

Is it better to pay off small debts or high interest first?

High interest first saves the most money; small balances first keeps more people in the game. The tracker's side-by-side plan pages let you run your own debts both ways and see the difference — then commit to the one you'll actually still be following at month six.
