Why Sunday evening works for ADHD planning

Monday-morning planning collides with the hardest executive-function moment of the week. Planning on Sunday, when the pressure is off, means decisions are already made when Monday shows up. The reset walks you through four moves:

  1. Brain dump — everything spinning in your head, onto one page (grab the free brain-dump template).
  2. Decide — pick the one thing that actually matters this week.
  3. Defer — explicitly park everything else where Future You can find it.
  4. Set the first move — one concrete action for Monday, so you start moving instead of deciding.

FAQ

How do I reset my week with ADHD?

Run a short Sunday ritual: dump every task onto one page, cross off anything that survived being ignored, shrink the rest to five-minute first steps, and assign exactly one anchor task per day. Pre-plan the comeback for the day after you slip. Thirty minutes, once a week — see the full ADHD weekly reset routine.

Why do weekly planners fail ADHD brains?

A weekly list front-loads every decision onto your lowest-energy day and has no recovery path after one missed day. The reset replaces the list with one anchor task per day and a written comeback plan, so a bad Tuesday doesn't cancel the week.

What is an ADHD weekly reset?

A short planning ritual done at the same time every week — usually Sunday evening — where you empty your head onto one page, choose a single priority, park the rest, and set one concrete first move for Monday. It replaces the long weekly to-do list that ADHD brains tend to abandon by Wednesday.

What should be on an ADHD weekly reset checklist?

Four moves: a brain dump of everything on your mind; a decide step choosing the single thing that matters most this week; a defer step parking everything else where future you can find it; and one concrete first action for Monday. One anchor task per day plus a written comeback plan complete the checklist.

How long does a weekly reset take?

About an hour, once a week, ideally Sunday evening. Most of the time goes to the brain dump and choosing the week's priority — and once it's a ritual, it runs on habit instead of willpower.
