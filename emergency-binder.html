The family emergency binder: everything important, one place, findable in a crisis

When something goes wrong, the information your family needs is scattered — insurance policies in an email account, account numbers in one partner's password manager, and the wishes nobody wrote down. This binder gathers all of it into one findable place.

What's inside

  • Documents — what each document is, where the original lives, and who needs a copy: IDs, policies, deeds, medical records.
  • Accounts — the full financial picture on one page per person: what exists, who owns it, how it's accessed.
  • Wishes — the section most families never write: what you'd want done, who decides what, and the conversations this page spares everyone.

The point of paper

In a crisis, nobody wants to learn a password manager or search an inbox. A binder on a known shelf works for whoever needs it — including the family member who isn't the family's accountant. Printable PDF, instant download.

What should be in a family emergency binder?

Eight sections cover it: identities and key documents, insurance policies, financial accounts, property records, pet care instructions, medical basics, end-of-life wishes, and a first-72-hours one-pager. The sections almost everyone forgets are passwords and digital accounts, and telling someone where the binder lives. The printable binder includes all sections as fill-in pages.

Where should you keep an emergency binder?

One obvious physical location — a specific shelf or safe the household knows — plus one backup copy stored separately, such as at a relative's home. Then tell at least one trusted person outside the household where it is; a binder nobody can find helps nobody. Update it once a year or after any big life change.

Is a paper or digital emergency binder better?

Both, on purpose. Paper works during a power outage and is instantly grab-and-go; a digital copy survives fire and flood. The practical setup is a paper binder as the master, plus one encrypted digital backup. Never store the binder's contents online unencrypted, and never publish your own details.
