A burnout recovery workbook: the check, the energy audit and the 6-week ladder

Burnout advice tends to be "take a vacation" — which doesn't fix the structure that exhausted you. Recovery needs a diagnosis first: what exactly is draining the tank, and a climb back that doesn't rely on willpower you don't have.

What's inside

  • The Check — a honest self-assessment of where you actually are: cynicism, exhaustion, efficacy. You can't recover from a level you won't name.
  • The Energy Audit — one week of logging what gave energy and what drained it, across work, people, home and body. The drains are usually specific, and specific things can be changed.
  • The 6-Week Ladder — one recovery rung a week: boundaries, sleep, delegation, recovery time that's actually scheduled — sequenced so each week is survivable.

Who it's for

Anyone running on fumes who can't just quit — employees, caregivers, founders, parents. It works alongside medical care, not instead of it. Printable PDF, instant download.

How long does burnout recovery take?

Weeks for lighter burnout, months for deep burnout — the honest range, and anyone promising days is selling something. The useful test is whether your energy survives a normal week, not whether you had one good day. The workbook's weekly check tracks exactly that, so you can see the trend instead of guessing.

Can you recover from burnout without quitting your job?

Often yes, but not by resting harder inside the same load — something structural has to change: boundaries, task volume, or support. Recovery is subtraction first. The workbook's energy audit shows you where the drain actually is, and its ladder rebuilds capacity in steps that survive a bad week at the same job.

What is the first step to recover from burnout?

A check, not a change. Before adding routines or quitting anything, spend one week recording where energy goes and what returns any. Most people find one or two drains they can cut immediately. The workbook opens with exactly that check, then builds the 6-week ladder on what it finds.
