Catch bad DTF files before they hit your film

Every DTF print shop knows the moment: the file looked fine on screen, the customer approved the mockup, and the print comes out fuzzy, translucent, or with whites gone transparent. That's a wasted film, a wasted press, and a reprint for free.

What actually goes wrong with DTF files

  • Transparent whites — white at 0% opacity vanishes on film and the shirt shows through.
  • Fuzzy edges — low-res art scaled up, or vector strokes too thin to hold powder.
  • Missing traps — colors that butt against each other with a hairline gap that opens on press.
  • Wrong DPI or size — the file prints at half the garment size, or softens at 3x.
