Puppy training schedule by age — feeding, sleep, potty and training from 8 weeks to 12 months

Last updated: November 12, 2026

A new 8-week-old puppy arrives and the first question is not "what tricks should we teach" — it's "what is our day even supposed to look like?" Here's the schedule by age: one rhythm per life stage, feeding to potty to naps to short training slots. Everything below is a labeled rule of thumb to adjust to your puppy, not a vet prescription.

8–10 weeks: the everything-is-new stage

The day is a loop: wake → potty outside → feed → play 10 minutes → potty again → nap. Puppies this age sleep most of the day, and an overtired puppy bites harder — enforced naps aren't spoiling, they're management. Potty breaks every 1–2 hours awake, always after eating, playing or waking. Training is minutes a day: their name, the potty spot, and the crate as the best place in the house.

3 months: the socialization window

Feeds drop to three, stretches between potty breaks lengthen, and the crate rhythm sets: wake, potty, train, play, nap, repeat. This stage has one job vets and trainers agree on — gentle, positive exposure to new people, surfaces, sounds and handling while the window is wide open. Keep sessions two to five minutes.

4–6 months: adolescence starts

Feeds settle to two or three, naps consolidate, and the leash walk becomes a daily thing — loose-leash practice starts now, before pulling is a habit. The headline of this stage: training regression is normal. The puppy that "had" sit suddenly doesn't. That's adolescence, not failure — go back to short sessions and high-value rewards without panicking.

6–12 months: consolidation and the teenage months

Two feeds, adult sleep patterns, and bladder capacity that finally holds through the night for most puppies. Adolescence peaks somewhere in this window — recall gets selective, and the answer is more practice in boring places, not longer leash. By twelve months the daily rhythm you built is simply "the routine," and the year's logs (vaccines, weights, milestones) are the record every future vet visit wants.

FAQ

What is a good schedule for an 8-week-old puppy?

A workable default loop: wake → potty → feed → 10 minutes of play → potty → nap, repeating all day with a final potty before bed. Expect 3–4 feeds, naps most of the day, and potty breaks every 1–2 hours while awake. Adjust to your puppy — the loop matters more than the exact times.

How long can a puppy hold its bladder?

The common rule of thumb is roughly one hour per month of age, plus one — a 3-month-old might hold it about four hours at most. Start lower and succeed more: an accident-free record builds the habit faster than testing the maximum.

How much do puppies sleep?

A lot: 18–20 hours a day at 8–10 weeks, settling toward 12–16 hours by adolescence. Overtired puppies bite and melt down more, so scheduled naps in the crate help behavior as much as rest. If your puppy is a terror at 7pm, the cause is usually missing naps, not attitude.

When should puppies start training?

The day they come home. Eight-week-old puppies learn name recognition, potty spot and crate association immediately — sessions of one to three minutes, many times a day. Formal obedience classes can start once the vet confirms vaccine timing; the foundation starts now, free.
