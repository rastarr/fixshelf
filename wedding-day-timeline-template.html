What time should you start getting ready?

For most weddings, hair and makeup start 3–4 hours before the ceremony for the couple, and the wedding party joins about 90 minutes in. The buffer isn't beauty time — it absorbs the delayed makeup artist, the missing groomsman and the "just one more photo" that always happens.

Assumptions: ceremony and reception at one venue, couple on-site, roughly 4 bridesmaids and 4 groomsmen. Moving between sites? Add about 30 minutes per move, plus the buffer below. Rule of thumb, not a law — your vendors' quoted times override this table.

The five things that break a wedding timeline

Photography overruns. Family formals balloon when the list isn't fixed in advance. Fix: hand your photographer a named list — 10 combos, not 25 — and a hard stop time.

Vendor load-in. The florist, DJ and caterer all arrive in the same hour. Fix: staggered load-in windows in the vendor sheet, and someone assigned to open the door.

Bridal-party size. Each extra person adds real minutes to hair, makeup and photos. Fix: scale the "start getting ready" time by party size, not by hope.

Travel between sites. Ceremony church, photos park, reception hall — each move costs 30 minutes plus rounding-up-the-party time. Fix: one venue, or one bus.

"Just one more family photo." Not a joke — it's the most common 20-minute leak in a reception. Fix: a designated family-photo wrangler with the list, who isn't you.

FAQ

What time should a wedding day start getting ready?

Start hair and makeup 3–4 hours before the ceremony for the couple, with the wedding party joining about 90 minutes in. The point of the early start is buffer for delays, not extra mirror time — the schedule that survives is the one with slack built in before the ceremony, not after.

What is a good wedding day timeline for a 4pm ceremony?

Workable default: hair and makeup at 11am, dress on 1:30pm, first look 2:15pm, guests at 3pm, ceremony at 4pm, cocktail hour 4:45pm with couple photos at sunset, dinner at 6pm, toasts at 8pm, last dance 11:30pm. Adjust for party size and travel.

How long should you allow for wedding photos?

A workable default: 45–60 minutes for family formals during cocktail hour and 20–30 minutes for couple photos, ideally at golden hour. Budget the time in the timeline itself — photos that "just happen" between events are the ones that overrun.

How much buffer time does a wedding timeline need?

Plan for one 20–30 minute buffer before the ceremony and at least one mid-reception buffer, placed where a delay does the least damage. If the day has no slack anywhere, the first late vendor pushes dinner, toasts and the venue cutoff all at once.
