The Christmas planner: a 6-week countdown that gets December organised, not survived

Christmas doesn't go wrong on the day — it goes wrong in the six weeks before it, quietly: gifts bought at panic prices, the food shop duplicated, the budget a mystery until the January statement. This planner runs the whole six weeks on paper.

What's inside

  • The 6-week countdown — week-by-week tasks that front-load what can be done early: cards, decorations, non-perishables, the big-ticket gifts.
  • The gift tracker with budget — every person, every gift, every amount on one page, so the total is known before the receipts are.
  • The food plan — menu, consolidated shopping list and the cooking schedule for the day itself, so the oven has a plan too.

Why six weeks

Six weeks is the window where Christmas is still cheap: gifts bought in November cost less and ship calmly, food can be bought across several normal shops instead of one panic trip, and nothing needs a rush fee. Starting at six weeks is what makes the countdown gentle instead of frantic. Printable PDF, instant download.

When should you start planning for Christmas?

Six weeks out is the practical answer: early enough that gifts arrive without rush fees and the food budget spreads across normal shops, late enough that the plan still feels real. The planner's countdown starts there — week one is cards and decorations, the panic-prone jobs go last, and the food plan locks in the final fortnight.

How do you budget for Christmas gifts?

Decide the total first, then split it per person before a single gift is bought — and write every purchase down as it happens. The gift tracker's budget column does exactly this: a known total, a per-person allocation, and a running record so December 24th never surprises the January statement.
