ADHD brain dump template — dump it, sort it into three zones, pick a top 3

Last updated: November 12, 2026

A brain dump is the fastest way to turn 2am mental static into a piece of paper — and it's one of the few techniques that needs no discipline, no app and no routine to work. The catch: most advice stops at "write it all down." An undifferentiated pile of 40 items is its own new anxiety. The three-zone method below ends the dump with a decision, not a pile. Paper and pen; that's the whole toolkit.

What a brain dump does for an ADHD brain

Working memory is the brain's scratchpad — what it holds at once — and ADHD brains run it with less headroom. Every open loop (the unpaid bill, the email you owe, the thing you meant to look up) sits in that scratchpad demanding attention, which is why the head feels loud and starting anything feels impossible. Writing items down isn't organization; it's offloading — each line on paper stops paying rent in your head. That's why a dump feels physically quieter.

Zone 1: The dump (5 minutes, no sorting)

Set a timer for five minutes and write everything in your head — tasks, worries, ideas, that thing you need to remember to buy, the awkward conversation. Rules: no sorting, no judging, no organizing. Sorting mid-dump is what makes people quit; a dump's only job is offloading. Messy handwriting, arrows, duplicates — all fine.

Zone 2: The sort (now / today / someday)

Go down the pile and mark each item with one of three labels:

  • Now — it's genuinely blocking something or due within hours.
  • Today — it should happen today or this week.
  • Someday — real, but not this week. Onto a someday page it goes, out of your head and out of today's sight.

Most of a typical dump lands in Someday — which is the point. The head was treating forty someday-items as active emergencies; the sort corrects the accounting.

Zone 3: The top 3

From Now and Today, circle three items. Not five — three is a list a bad day can finish. Each circled item gets the same treatment as the four-column breakdown: one visible five-minute first move. The dump now ends with a decision — three startable moves — instead of a pile.

Use it even if you never print anything

Any paper works: one page, three zones, a timer. The value is the loop, not the stationery. FAQ

What is a brain dump for ADHD?

Writing everything in your head onto one page — tasks, worries, ideas — in one unsorted five-minute pass, then sorting it into now / today / someday and circling a top 3. It works because ADHD working memory is overloaded: each item on paper stops consuming the headroom the brain needs to actually start things.

How do I do a brain dump with ADHD?

Timer for five minutes, write everything without sorting or judging, then sort the pile into three zones — now, today, someday — and circle three items, each with one visible five-minute first move. The no-sorting rule matters most: sorting mid-dump is where ADHD brain dumps usually die.

Is a brain dump the same as journaling?

No. Journaling processes feelings and reflections in sentences; a brain dump offloads open loops as fragments in five minutes and ends with a sorted list and a top 3. Journaling asks "how do I feel about this?" — a dump asks "what's still in my head?" Both help; they solve different problems.
