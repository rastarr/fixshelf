Menopause symptom tracker printable — what to record, and what to bring your doctor

Last updated: November 12, 2026

"How have your symptoms been?" is the hardest question in menopause care, because the honest answer is usually a shrug. Months of hot flashes compress into "a lot?", sleep trouble blurs together, and the appointment happens before you've found the words. The fix is unglamorous: track for 90 days, and walk in with evidence instead of a shrug. One line first — this page helps you record and communicate symptoms; it doesn't diagnose anything or replace medical advice.

What symptoms are worth tracking

  • Hot flashes — how many per day, and how severe (1–10).
  • Night sweats — nights per week you wake soaked.
  • Sleep — time to fall asleep, night wakes, how rested you feel (1–10).
  • Mood — anxiety, irritability, low days; a one-word day label is enough.
  • Brain fog — the days you couldn't find words or lost a thread mid-sentence.
  • Cycles — start date, length, heaviness; perimenopause is often defined by the change in these.
  • Suspected triggers — coffee, alcohol, stress, heat, poor sleep — noted alongside, not judged yet.

How to track so a doctor can use the data

Four fields per day, two minutes: symptom, severity 1–10, possible trigger, date. That's the whole method. The weekly summary is where patterns surface — "hot flashes peaked the week I restarted coffee" is a sentence your doctor can act on; "it's been rough lately" is not.

Perimenopause symptoms fluctuate on long cycles, which is why the 90-day window matters: a bad week looks like a trend, a good week looks like recovery, and only a quarter of data shows the real pattern underneath both.

The 90-day printable tracker

The Menopause Symptom Tracker printable is this method made usable: a daily log, a trigger finder that surfaces patterns after a few weeks, and a one-page doctor visit summary you fill in from the data instead of from memory.

Bringing your tracker to the appointment

Highlight three things the night before: your worst symptom (with frequency and severity), your biggest change since last visit, and the one question you most want answered. Hand over the summary page rather than reciting from memory — the doctor reads faster than you talk, and the page becomes part of your record.

Printable tracker or app?

Stated criteria: convenience, visibility, and handover. Apps win convenience — the phone is always with you. Printables win the other two: the log on the fridge is visible daily (apps hide in folders), and handing a doctor a printed page beats handing them your phone. Many people use both; the printable is the one that reaches the appointment.

FAQ

What is the best way to track menopause symptoms?

A daily log with four fields — symptom, severity 1–10, possible trigger, date — plus a weekly summary. Ninety days of that shows frequency, severity and patterns that weekly memory can't. The output that matters is a one-page summary you can hand your doctor at the appointment.

How do I track perimenopause symptoms for my doctor?

Record each symptom's frequency and severity with dates, keep a simple cycle log, and bring a one-page written summary to the appointment. Doctors act on frequency, severity and change-over-time — the three things memory is worst at reconstructing.

Is there a free menopause symptom tracker?

The method on this page is free to use with any notebook: symptom, severity, trigger, date, daily. The 90-day printable adds a structured daily log, a trigger finder and the doctor-visit summary page — the parts that are tedious to build yourself.

What symptoms should I track during perimenopause?

Hot flashes, night sweats, sleep quality, mood changes, brain fog, and your cycle dates and heaviness — plus suspected triggers like caffeine, alcohol and stress. Tracking all of them daily at low effort beats tracking one perfectly.
