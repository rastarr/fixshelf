A pregnancy checklist by trimester — and the phases after

The first trimester checklist runs while everything is new; the registry guide lands in month four or five when the stuff decisions peak; the recovery checklist is printed and ready before the due date, because nobody plans the fourth trimester from a hospital bed. Printable PDF, instant download.

What should you do first when you find out you're pregnant?

The first trimester checklist opens with the actual first moves: book the first appointment, start the prenatal vitamin routine, note which current medications need a conversation, and line up the paperwork. Everything else — registry, nursery, birth plan — has its own phase later, and the checklist tells you which one.

What do you actually need on a baby registry?

Fewer things than registries suggest: safe sleep, safe travel, feeding supplies, nappies and weather-appropriate clothing cover about ninety percent of the first months. The registry guide's category checklists walk each one, and its price ladder shows where spending more buys real safety versus marketing.

What is the fourth trimester and how do you plan for it?

The twelve weeks after birth, when recovery, feeding and sleep deprivation all peak together. Planning it means deciding recovery milestones, who handles what, a feeding log and mood check-ins before the baby arrives — which is what the postpartum recovery checklist is for. It's the phase almost nobody prepares, and the easiest to plan from a hospital bed is never.
