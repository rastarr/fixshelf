Can ChatGPT help with ADHD planning?

Yes — usefully, if you keep it to jobs a chatbot is actually good at. It's a decent sorting engine for the brain dump, and a decent shrinker for tasks you can't start. Prompts that genuinely work:

  • The sort: "Here is my unsorted brain dump: [paste]. Sort these into now / today / someday. Don't delete anything and don't reword them — just sort."
  • The shrink: "Turn each of these into a task I can start in under 5 minutes, and tell me what the very first visible action is: [paste]."
  • The comeback: "I planned 6 things today and did 1. Pick the 2 that actually matter for tomorrow and tell me what to ignore."

Print the sampler pages as the destination for that output: the dump page holds the sorted list, the breakdown sheet holds the shrunk tasks. Paper survives the tab-switch; the chat scroll doesn't.

FAQ

Are free printable ADHD planners any good?

The good ones are narrow. A free planner works when it does one job per page — a dump page, a reset page — rather than trying to be a full life-management system across 40 sheets. Judge a free pack by whether you could print one page and use it today without reading instructions.

What pages should an ADHD planner have?

At minimum: a brain dump page (get it out of your head), a weekly reset page (shrink the week to one anchor per day), and a comeback page (recover from missed days without scrapping the week). A task breakdown sheet is the next addition — it turns avoided tasks into five-minute starts. Apps and fancy layouts are optional; those four jobs aren't.

Can I use ChatGPT to help me plan with ADHD printables?

Yes — as a sorter and a shrinker, not a planner. Have it sort your brain dump into now/today/someday, shrink big tasks into five-minute starts, or pick tomorrow's two things after a blown day. Then write the results onto the printable pages, where they stay visible instead of scrolling away.
