Can you train a reactive dog at home?

Yes for mild-to-moderate reactivity: the core work is finding the distance where your dog can still think and rewarding calm there, then shrinking that distance gradually. Severe reactivity — biting, injuries, or no safe distance at all — needs a certified force-free trainer alongside any home plan. The roadmap includes the "when to call a trainer" checklist.

What causes leash reactivity in dogs?

Most commonly fear, frustration, or a learned history — the dog barks because it worked, or because the thing on the other end scared them. Reactivity is not dominance. Understanding which driver your dog has changes which exercise you start with, which is why the roadmap opens with a trigger log before any training plan.

How long does reactive dog training take?

Months of consistent, short sessions is the honest default — progress shows up as a bigger working distance, not a cured dog. A six-week plan builds the habit and the tracking; some dogs move faster and some need a trainer's help. Every walk is data, which is what the trigger log is for.
